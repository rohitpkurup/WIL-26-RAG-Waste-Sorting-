TITLE: Changes to household waste and recycling in Victoria - the 4-stream system
SOURCE ORGANISATION: Department of Energy, Environment and Climate Action (DEECA), Victorian Government
SOURCE PAGE: Changes to household waste and recycling in Victoria
SOURCE URL: https://www.environment.vic.gov.au/household-waste-recycling/kerbside-reform
LAST UPDATED: 24 September 2026

Victoria is moving every council to a standard 4-stream household waste and recycling system by 2030. The four streams are food and garden organics (FOGO) with a lime green lid, glass recycling with a purple lid, mixed recycling with a yellow lid, and general rubbish with a red lid.

The general rubbish bin has a red lid. Everything in the general rubbish bin goes straight to landfill without sorting. Councils pay a landfill levy on this waste, so keeping food scraps, garden clippings and recyclables out of the red-lid bin reduces costs for ratepayers.

The mixed recycling bin has a yellow lid and takes accepted plastic, metal, paper and cardboard items.

So far 65 of Victoria's 79 councils have introduced a FOGO service. Food scraps and garden clippings make up about half of what is in a general rubbish bin. Once a FOGO service is available in an area, food scraps and garden clippings are no longer accepted in the general rubbish bin.

So far 43 councils have introduced a separate glass recycling service, and 27 of them provide a purple-lidded kerbside glass bin. Once a separate glass service is available in an area, glass is no longer accepted in the mixed recycling bin.

Apartments and multi-unit buildings often use a private waste provider instead of the council service. Residents there should ask their building manager, owners corporation or waste provider what applies.

Victoria's target is to divert 80% of waste from landfill by 2030.
