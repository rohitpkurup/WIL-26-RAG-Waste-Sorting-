TITLE: Recycling food and garden organics in Victoria
SOURCE ORGANISATION: Department of Energy, Environment and Climate Action (DEECA), Victorian Government
SOURCE PAGE: Recycling food and garden organics in Victoria
SOURCE URL: https://www.environment.vic.gov.au/household-waste-recycling/recycling-food-garden-organics
LAST UPDATED: 24 September 2026

The FOGO bin takes food scraps and garden clippings. Bones, citrus peel and onion peel are accepted in FOGO bins, even though they are not suitable for home compost systems. Exactly what is accepted varies by council, so check your council's website.

Never put these in the FOGO bin: fruit stickers, food packaging such as plastic wrapping and meat trays, cling wrap, resealable sandwich bags, containers, and takeaway packaging and utensils. This includes takeaway items labelled compostable, such as bamboo, sugarcane, cardboard or bioplastic cups, bowls, boxes, straws and cutlery. If packaging and other rubbish go into the FOGO bin, the compost becomes contaminated and may be sent to landfill.

To use the FOGO bin well, collect scraps in a small kitchen caddy, such as a reused ice cream tub, and empty it into the FOGO bin when full. To reduce smells and pests, keep the bin in the shade with the lid closed, freeze smelly scraps and add them the day before collection, and layer garden clippings between food scraps.

Food scraps and garden clippings should only go in the general rubbish bin if you have no FOGO bin and no composting system such as a compost bin, worm farm or bokashi system. In landfill, organic material produces methane, a greenhouse gas.

By 2030 all Victorians with council waste services will have a lime green-lidded FOGO bin or a communal drop-off location.
