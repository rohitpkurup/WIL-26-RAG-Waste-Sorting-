TITLE: How to sort waste and recycling at home - materials guide
SOURCE ORGANISATION: Department of Energy, Environment and Climate Action (DEECA), Victorian Government
SOURCE PAGE: How to sort waste and recycling at home
SOURCE URL: https://www.environment.vic.gov.au/household-waste-recycling/sort-waste-recycling
LAST UPDATED: 24 September 2026

Metal: food cans, drink cans, aluminium trays and aluminium foil go in the mixed recycling bin. Scrunch aluminium foil into a ball at least the size of a tennis ball first. Empty and rinse cans and keep them loose, not in bags. Pots, pans, cutlery and other metal household items are not accepted in the mixed recycling bin; donate usable ones, and put unusable ones in the general rubbish bin.

Paper and cardboard go in the mixed recycling bin. Remove food, flatten boxes and keep paper dry. Paper towels, tissues, serviettes and food-soiled or oily paper and cardboard, such as a greasy pizza box, are not accepted in mixed recycling. For shredded paper and wrapping paper, check your council's rules.

Hard plastics commonly accepted in mixed recycling include milk, juice and water bottles, yoghurt and ice cream tubs, fruit punnets, biscuit trays, and shampoo and soap bottles. Rules for hard plastics vary between councils because recycling facilities accept different materials. Empty and rinse containers and keep them loose, never inside plastic bags.

Soft plastics are plastics you can scrunch into a ball, such as plastic bags, chip packets and the outer wrappers of biscuit packets. Soft plastics generally go in the general rubbish bin. Some councils run soft plastic kerbside trials or drop-off points, and a growing number of supermarkets collect soft plastics again.

Clothing and textiles must not go in recycling bins because they get caught in equipment at recycling facilities. Donate, give away or sell clothing in good condition, repair items with minor damage, turn damaged items into cleaning rags, or use a textile collection service or drop-off point.

Recycling symbols: the Mobius loop of three arrows does not always mean an item is accepted in household recycling bins. Plastic codes 1 and 2 are commonly accepted, code 5 is accepted by some councils, and codes 3, 4, 6 and 7 are less commonly accepted. On the Australasian Recycling Label, a solid recycling symbol means recycling bin, a transparent recycling symbol means follow the directions (often store drop-off), and a bin symbol means general rubbish bin.

Container deposit scheme: eligible glass, plastic and metal drink containers can be returned for a 10-cent refund through CDS Vic.
