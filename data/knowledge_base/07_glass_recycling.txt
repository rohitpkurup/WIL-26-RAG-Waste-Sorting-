TITLE: Recycling glass in Victoria
SOURCE ORGANISATION: Department of Energy, Environment and Climate Action (DEECA), Victorian Government
SOURCE PAGE: Recycling glass in Victoria
SOURCE URL: https://www.environment.vic.gov.au/household-waste-recycling/recycling-glass
LAST UPDATED: 24 September 2026

Glass bottles and jars from food, drinks and medicines can be recycled, for example pasta sauce jars, pickle jars, peanut butter jars and beer bottles. Put them in the purple-lidded glass recycling bin if you have one. If your area does not have a glass bin yet, glass bottles and jars can go in the mixed recycling bin.

Before recycling glass bottles and jars, scrape out food and give them a quick rinse, and keep them loose rather than in bags. Whether lids stay on or come off depends on the council, so check your local council website.

Drinking glasses, mirrors, window glass, baking dishes, ceramics, crockery and perfume bottles cannot go in any recycling bin, including the glass bin, because they melt at a different temperature to bottles and jars. Put broken drinking glasses and similar household glassware in the general rubbish bin.

Glass is collected separately because small pieces of broken glass get stuck in paper, cardboard and plastic and spoil those materials. Making glass from recycled glass uses around 20 to 30 percent less energy than making it from virgin sand.
