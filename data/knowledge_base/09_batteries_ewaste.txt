TITLE: How to dispose of batteries and electronic waste in Victoria
SOURCE ORGANISATION: Department of Energy, Environment and Climate Action (DEECA), Victorian Government
SOURCE PAGE: How to dispose of batteries and electronic waste in Victoria
SOURCE URL: https://www.environment.vic.gov.au/household-waste-recycling/ewaste
LAST UPDATED: 24 September 2026

Never put batteries or electronics in any bin. In Victoria it is illegal to dispose of e-waste in any household or kerbside bin. Batteries can cause fires in collection trucks and recycling facilities, and they contain valuable materials such as copper that can be recovered.

E-waste is any item with a battery, plug or cord that no longer works. If a device lights up, makes a sound, moves or needs charging, it has an embedded battery and is e-waste.

Items with embedded batteries include bluetooth speakers and headphones, electric toothbrushes, e-scooters, e-bikes and hoverboards, flashing and light-up toys, electric shavers, power banks, remote-controlled and ride-on toys, cordless and robotic vacuum cleaners, vapes, smartwatches and fitness trackers, and power tools.

To store used batteries safely until you reach a drop-off point, cover the battery terminals with clear sticky tape to prevent sparks. Keep them in a glass or plastic container that is not airtight, such as a jam jar or ice cream tub with the lid off. Do not use a metal container or store batteries with metal objects. Keep them in a cool, dry place away from heat, children and pets.

Take batteries and e-waste to a drop-off point. Find one with the Victorian Government's Recycle Mate search tool or on your council's website.
