TITLE: How to dispose of hazardous household chemicals
SOURCE ORGANISATION: Department of Energy, Environment and Climate Action (DEECA), Victorian Government
SOURCE PAGE: How to dispose of hazardous household chemicals
SOURCE URL: https://www.environment.vic.gov.au/hazardous-household-chemicals/how-to-dispose-of-hazardous-household-chemicals
LAST UPDATED: 6 July 2026

Never put hazardous chemicals in a rubbish bin, where they can explode, catch fire, leak or mix with other chemicals. Never pour chemicals down the drain, where they pollute the environment and can contaminate the water supply.

Paint: take unwanted paint and paint packaging to a Paintback collection site. Paintback sites accept only paint, not other chemicals.

Medicines and pharmaceuticals: return unwanted or expired medicines to a local pharmacy through the Return Unwanted Medicines project.

Motor oil: take it to a council disposal point. Motor oil is not accepted at Detox Your Home events.

Gas bottles: some council transfer stations accept gas bottles; check a recycling locator.

Empty aerosol cans can go in the household mixed recycling bin.

Cooking oil: some council transfer stations accept it, and small amounts may be accepted in the FOGO bin if your council allows it.

Fluorescent lights: take them to a council waste and recycling centre for e-waste recycling.

Printer cartridges: use the Cartridges 4 Planet Ark program.

Mobile phones: recycle them through MobileMuster.

Car batteries: take them to a battery retailer or council waste centre. Household batteries: use a B-cycle drop-off point.

Vapes: check your council website or a recycling locator for a drop-off point.

Asbestos: follow the guidance on the Asbestos in Victoria website. Ammunition, marine flares, fireworks and explosives: call WorkSafe Victoria. Farm chemicals: use the ChemClear program.

Nail polish: leave the open bottle to harden, then put it in the rubbish bin.
